| **Portfolio** | 100 shares @ ₹1,320. Shares unchanged. avg_cost unchanged. Cash unchanged. | ✅ No corruption |
| **Result** | All prices in adjusted series match raw prices exactly. No modification. | ✅ Pass-through |

### Known Limitation

Dividend cash injection is **not implemented** in V1. The system:
- Logs a warning that the dividend is not modeled
- Does NOT inject `dividend_per_share × eligible_shares` into cash
- Does NOT adjust prices for total-return computation
- Returns are **price-only**, not total-return

This is documented and will not silently produce wrong results. When dividend injection is implemented, the test `test_dividend_cash_injection_not_implemented` should be updated.

## Double-Adjustment Safeguard — PASS ✅

| Scenario | Input | Output | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| RAW → adjust | ₹4,567.50 (raw) | ₹913.50 (correct) | ✅ |
| ALREADY ADJUSTED → adjust | ₹913.50 (yfinance) | ₹182.70 (WRONG) | ✅ Demonstrates danger |
| Auditability | `adjustment_factor` column | 0.2 pre-split, 1.0 post-split | ✅ Traceable |

### Critical Data Sourcing Finding

> **yfinance `auto_adjust=False` Close is ALREADY adjusted for splits and bonuses.**

This was verified empirically:
- IRCTC Oct 28 yfinance Close: ₹913.50. Real NSE raw close: ₹4,567.50 (= 913.50 × 5)
- TCS May 30 yfinance Close: ₹1,757.05. Real NSE raw close: ₹3,514.10 (= 1757.05 × 2)

The `Adj Close` column further adjusts for dividends, but `Close` is already split/bonus-adjusted. This means:

1. **yfinance "Close" cannot be treated as raw data** for our adjustment engine
2. Applying our adjustment on top of yfinance data would **double-adjust** (verified: produces ₹182.70 instead of ₹913.50)
3. For the engine to work correctly with yfinance, either:
   - Use yfinance data as-is (it's pre-adjusted, no engine adjustment needed), OR
   - Reconstruct raw prices by reversing the known adjustment factor

The current safeguard is the `adjustment_factor` auditability column. A future enhancement could add explicit `adjustment_status` metadata on the data source.

## Additional Finding: yfinance Ex-Date Discrepancy

| Security | Event | yfinance Event Date | NSE Ex-Date | Discrepancy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCS | 1:1 Bonus | 2018-05-31 | 2018-06-14 | **14 days** |
| IRCTC | 5:1 Split | 2021-10-28 | 2021-10-29 | **1 day** |

yfinance reports corporate-action dates that differ from NSE's official ex-dates. For TCS, the 14-day discrepancy is significant — it would cause the adjustment boundary to be applied to the wrong dates, producing wrong signals for ~2 weeks of data.

**Recommendation:** yfinance cannot be used as the authoritative source for ex-dates. NSE corporate-action announcements must be used as the source of truth. For V1, this means manual curation of the corporate-actions CSV.
